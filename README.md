**Project Status:** Completed

## 1. Executive Summary

This project presents an interactive business intelligence dashboard developed using Tableau Public to evaluate employee attrition and workforce characteristics within the IBM HR Analytics dataset. The analysis examines workforce composition, departmental turnover patterns, employee tenure, compensation, overtime and job satisfaction.

The dashboard identifies significant differences in observed attrition across employee groups. Employees reporting overtime demonstrate an attrition rate of 30.53%, compared with 10.44% among employees without overtime. Employees with less than three years of service also experience relatively high attrition at 29.82%, while Sales Representatives record the highest job-role attrition rate of 39.76%.

These findings highlight potential workforce retention concerns and provide an analytical foundation for investigating employee experience, workload distribution and compensation practices.

## 3. Business Problem and Analytical Objectives

Employee attrition can create recruitment costs, operational disruption, knowledge loss and workforce planning challenges. Understanding differences in employee departures across workforce segments can support more informed retention decisions.

This project addresses five analytical objectives:

1. Evaluate workforce composition and employee attrition.
2. Identify departments and job roles with higher observed attrition rates.
3. Examine relationships between employee tenure, compensation and departures.
4. Assess differences in attrition associated with overtime and employee satisfaction.
5. Translate analytical findings into practical workforce management recommendations.

## 5. Business Intelligence Analysis and Findings

### 5.1 Departmental Attrition Performance

| Department | Attrition Rate |
|---|---:|
| Sales | 20.63% |
| Human Resources | 19.05% |
| Research & Development | 13.84% |

**Analytical Finding**

Sales records the highest departmental attrition rate at 20.63%, exceeding Research & Development by 6.79 percentage points. Human Resources also demonstrates comparatively elevated attrition at 19.05%.

**Business Implication**

The differences suggest that employee retention concerns may vary across organisational functions. Department-specific investigations could help management understand whether workload, career progression, leadership practices or compensation contribute to these patterns.

### 5.2 Job Role Attrition Analysis

| Job Role | Attrition Rate |
|---|---:|
| Sales Representative | 39.76% |
| Laboratory Technician | 23.94% |
| Human Resources | 23.08% |
| Sales Executive | 17.48% |
| Research Scientist | 16.10% |
| Manufacturing Director | 6.90% |
| Healthcare Representative | 6.87% |
| Manager | 4.90% |
| Research Director | 2.50% |

**Analytical Finding**

Sales Representatives experience the highest observed attrition rate at 39.76%. Laboratory Technicians have an attrition rate of 23.94% and account for 62 recorded employee departures, the highest departure count among job roles.

**Business Implication**

The findings demonstrate the importance of examining both attrition percentages and departure volumes. Sales Representatives represent a high-rate retention concern, while Laboratory Technicians represent a substantial volume of employee departures.

Management could prioritise these groups for targeted workforce reviews.
